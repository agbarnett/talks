# Talk for the [Impact Evaluation Practitioners Network](https://evaluation.treasury.gov.au/learn-and-connect/impact-evaluation-practitioners-network)

*Title*: Lotteries for policy administration and evaluation

*Outline*: Lotteries have a long history of being used to make difficult decisions, including what men were conscripted into the army, and what children were admitted to London's foundling hospital. Lotteries create an almost perfect randomised controlled trial and hence can be used to create high quality evidence for policy evaluation. For example, medical students in Sweden have long been selected by lottery and a follow-up study of doctors and "controls" (often lawyers) shows a remarkable benefit in life expectancy for the family members of doctors. Our work on lotteries has focused on the difficult decision of what researchers to fund with over-subscribed funding systems using imperfect information. The number of research funders using a lottery is growing and this has created trials of the impact of funding. I will share the results of the funding lottery used by the New Zealand Health Research Council, and some preliminary results from a funding lottery by The British Academy.
